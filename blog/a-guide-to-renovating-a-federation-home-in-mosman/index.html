A Guide to Renovating a Federation Home in Mosman

Mosman has one of the richest concentrations of Federation architecture on Sydney's Lower North Shore, and renovating one of these homes well is a genuinely different exercise to renovating a newer property. Here is what we think about before the first wall comes down.

Understand What You're Working With

Before anything else, establish whether the property is heritage listed, sits within a conservation area, or has no formal protection at all. Each carries a different set of constraints on what council will and will not approve, and it shapes the whole renovation strategy from the outset. If you are still house hunting, our guide to what to look for when purchasing a Federation home is worth reading before you commit to a property.

Deciding What to Keep

Original features, cornicing, fireplaces, leadlight windows, wide floorboards, are rarely a problem to work around. They are usually the reason the home is worth renovating in the first place. On a recent Mosman Federation renovation we completed, we worked with a family who had owned their home since 1930. A curved front facade and an earlier rear extension had diluted the home's original character over the decades, so the brief became about restoring what had been lost as much as adding anything new.

Extending Without Losing the Original Home

Most Federation renovations in Mosman involve some form of extension, whether that is a new kitchen and living wing, an additional storey, or both. The homes we work on most successfully treat the addition as a clearly separate, contemporary chapter rather than an attempt to mimic the original architecture. On that same project, a bespoke arched steel door now marks the transition from the heritage front rooms to the new rear addition, a deliberate, confident line between old and new rather than a blurred one.

The contemporary rear addition on our Mosman Federation renovation, deliberately distinct from the restored heritage front of the home.

Choosing Materials That Respect the Period

Federation homes tend to reward restraint. Warm whites make a timeless, forgiving backdrop for original detailing, while black accents and custom joinery in oak or similar timbers introduce warmth without competing with the architecture. The goal is a material palette that feels considered and current, not a pastiche of the period, and not a stark departure from it either.

Working With the Right Team

Heritage-sensitive renovations usually involve a heritage draftsperson or architect working alongside your designer and builder, particularly where council approval is required for changes to a street-facing facade. Bringing your interior designer into that conversation early, rather than after the architectural plans are finalised, means the interior scheme and the structural changes can be developed together rather than one working around the other.
