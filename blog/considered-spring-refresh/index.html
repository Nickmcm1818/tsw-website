A Considered Spring Refresh

There's something about spring that makes us want to fling open the windows and start afresh. As the days become lighter and longer and heavy winter layers are packed away, our homes can benefit from a seasonal reset, too. The good news? Giving your home a fresh look for spring doesn't require an expensive renovation or complete decorating overhaul. A few clever changes to colour, texture, light and styling can transform how a room feels.

A dining table dressed simply for spring with single-stem vases and the beautiful patterned wallpaper doing the rest. Image via Architectural Digest.

Clear Before You Style

Before anything new comes into a room, something needs to leave it. Clear the surfaces back to items you genuinely love, not the six you've collected! Store away the heavier winter pieces, the wool throws, the darker cushions, the candles that suited colder darker days, and let the room breathe before you start adding anything back. Negative space is doing as much work in a room as the objects that are sitting in it, and a refresh only works if there is room left for pieces to be seen.

A room that earns its clutter - full shelves and a beautifully worn table, in this reading room. Image via betsybrowninc.net.

Let the Light Back In

Coming out of winter, most homes are still dressed for it, heavier curtains, block-out linings, windows that haven't been properly cleaned since autumn. Start there. Clean the windows and look at your coverings. A sheer layer, or curtains pulled fully back during the day, will bring in far more daylight than you would expect, and daylight does more for the feel of a room than any amount of styling. Even repositioning furniture that blocks a window can make a surprising difference to how bright and airy a room feels.

A simple sheer blind softly frames this window whilst still allowing light to flood in to the room. Image via betsybrowninc.net.

Change the Weight of the Room

Much the same as we change our wardrobes with the seasons, our homes can benefit from a textile switch up as well. Pack away the chunky throws, heavy blankets and cushions and introduce breathable fabrics such as linen and cotton instead. You don't need to replace everything: simply changing a few cushion covers or the throw at the end of your bed can alter the feel of the room.

In the bedroom, lighter bed linen instantly creates that breezy, relaxed feeling that comes with warmer weather. Crisp white always works, but soft greens and blues and delicate patterns can bring a subtle sense of spring. It's a small, inexpensive change, and one of the fastest ways to make a room feel fresh again.

Crisp white linen against a bold Christoper Farr fabric headboard, and a simple jar of garden flowers completes the bedside. Image via Arent & Pyke.

Embrace Spring Colours

Resist the urge to bring in a whole new palette. The garden outside is doing the colour work at this time of year: sage greens, warm neutrals, pale pink, a pale sky blue or a sunny yellow all reference the season. If your home has a largely neutral palette, accessories are an easy place to experiment. A colourful cushion, patterned lampshade, piece of art or ceramic vase can be enough to breathe new life into a space. The trick is restraint.

Muted shades of pink and taupe along with butter yellow give a gentle nod to the season. Image via Hale Mercantile.

Refresh with Natural Textures

Texture can completely change the mood of a room, particularly when you swap heavier winter materials for relaxed natural fibres.

A jute or woven rug can create an easy, laid-back foundation for spring and summer, while baskets, linen, timber, rattan and ceramics bring another layer of warmth and tactility. You can also use rugs to introduce colour or pattern without committing to a permanent change. If you already have carpet, layering a contrasting rug over the top can help define a living zone while adding extra interest.

Woven baskets and pale linen bring natural texture into a quiet, all-white room. Image via Pinterest.

Bring the Garden Inside

Fresh greenery is one of the quickest ways to make a room feel alive again and your garden is the perfect place to find it. You don't need elaborate florist arrangements for a spring home refresh. A few branches cut from the garden can look spectacular in an oversized vase, while small posies in glass jars have an effortless appeal.

Indoor plants also come into their own at this time of year. Giving existing plants a little attention by removing dead leaves, wiping away dust and checking whether they need repotting or feeding. Then think beyond obvious places. A small vase beside the bed, a trailing plant on a bathroom shelf or pot of herbs in the kitchen can bring a welcome hit of green to hardworking spaces.

Garden branches used to dramatic effect in these spaces - a viburnum arrangement anchoring an antique table, and blossom brightens a dark butlers pantry. Images via betsybrowninc.net.

Restyle What You Already Own

The last step costs nothing at all. Take everything off the console or the coffee table and put back only what deserves to be there, varying height and texture. Move a lamp, swap an artwork from one room to another or relocate a vase that has sat in the same spot for years. Aim for a mixture of heights, shapes and textures rather than filling every available centimetre. Leaving some negative space will make treasured objects stand out and can help the entire room feel calmer. This is an exercise we run on most of our projects, styling with what's already in the house, and it's the easiest place for anyone to start on their own.

Finally, throw open the windows, let the breeze through - sometimes a fresh start is all your home needs!

Rattan chairs and a fresh upholstery fabric by Seema Krish give this room a bright and airy coastal feel. Image via Ann Prince Interiors.

A potted philodendron and a collection of simple objects are enough to make a table feel finished. Image via Pinterest.
