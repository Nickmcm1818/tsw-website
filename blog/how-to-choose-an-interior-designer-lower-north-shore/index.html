How to Choose an Interior Designer on the Lower North Shore

The Lower North Shore has no shortage of talented designers, which is exactly what makes choosing one difficult. Beyond a nice Instagram feed, the right fit comes down to a handful of practical questions that are worth asking before you commit to anyone.

Start With Portfolio Fit, Not Just Style

Every designer's portfolio will look beautiful. The more useful question is whether the work reflects the kind of project you actually have. A studio known for full renovations may not be the right fit for a single room refresh, and vice versa. Look for evidence of projects similar in scale, property type and budget to your own, not just images that appeal to your taste.

Ask How They Structure Fees

A good designer should be able to explain their pricing clearly and without hesitation. Some charge hourly, some work on fixed-fee packages, and most add a procurement margin on furniture and finishes sourced through the trade. None of these models is inherently better, but you should know exactly what you are paying for before any work begins. If a designer is vague about cost at the enquiry stage, that is worth noting.

Local Experience Matters More Than You'd Think

Lower North Shore homes bring their own particular set of considerations: harbour light that behaves differently to direct sun, a mix of Federation, Edwardian and mid-century architecture, and council and heritage requirements that vary from one municipality to the next. A designer who works regularly across Mosman, Cremorne, Cammeray, Neutral Bay, North Sydney and Crows Nest will already understand the light, the period detailing and the trades who do this kind of work well, rather than arriving at the area cold.

Understand the Process Before You Commit

Ask what happens after you sign on. A clear, structured process, discovery, concept, refinement, delivery, gives you confidence about what to expect and when. We have written in more detail about what happens at each stage of a TSW project, and any designer worth engaging should be able to walk you through their own version just as clearly.

Chemistry Is Not a Nice-to-Have

You will be making a lot of decisions together, sometimes over many months. Beyond credentials and portfolio, pay attention to how a designer listens, whether they ask genuine questions about how you live, and whether you feel comfortable being honest with them about budget and taste. The best working relationships are built on trust, not just talent.

If you are weighing up your options, an Initial Consultation is a low-commitment way to see how a studio works before deciding on anything more involved.
