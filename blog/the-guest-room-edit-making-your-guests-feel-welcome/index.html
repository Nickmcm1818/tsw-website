The Guest Room Edit: Making Your Guests Feel Welcome

The festive season brings family and friends into your home, which can mean opening up the guest room for the first time in months. Often it has inadvertently become a storage room, a home office, or the place the ironing gets left.

Getting it ready often doesn’t require major work. A guest room works when it has been thought through from the point of view of the person staying in it. A few considered changes make the difference between a spare room and a room you are happy to have your guests retreat to.

Shutters to control the light, a lamp for reading and a carafe within reach make this guest room feel thought through.

Start by spending a night in it

The best test of a guest room is to sleep in it yourself. You will notice very quickly what is missing: the lamp that is too low to read by, the curtain that lets the light in at 5am, nowhere to put a phone or a glass of water. Make a list of what’s missing and work through it.

Pare it back

Before adding anything, take things away. Guests should be able to unpack with ease, so clear part of the wardrobe and a couple of drawers, and leave plenty of good hangers. If the room doubles as a study, pack away the office stuff so the desk becomes a dressing table. A calm space is an edited one.

A simple, colourful room shows how much a calm, edited room can say. Image via Anna Spiro Design.

Invest in a good bed

The bed can make or break a guest room, so buy well here. Good quality linen or cotton sheets, a mattress protector and feather topper for softness, and two types of pillow, one soft and one firm, cover most preferences. In an Australian summer, swap the heavy quilt for a lightweight cotton blanket and coverlet and fold a throw at the end of the bed for cooler nights. Layer the bed with simple cushions, less is more here.

Layers folded across the foot of the bed add warmth for cooler nights, whilst keeping everything else simple. Image via Handelsmann + Khaw.

Get the bedside right

Every guest needs a surface on each side of the bed, a lamp they can actually read by, and somewhere to charge a phone. A carafe and glass, a small dish for a watch or jewellery, and one or two books chosen with your guest in mind finish it. Keep the arrangement simple. A bedside table crowded with decorative objects leaves no room for the things people bring with them.

A simple bedside with a lamp within easy reach and room for the things guests bring with them. Image via Pinterest.

Think about light and air

Lighting should be layered, not a single pendant overhead. A bedside lamp and a second soft source, such as a floor or table lamp, make the room usable at night without the main light on. Check the window treatments too. A blockout lining, or a sheer behind a heavier curtain, gives guests control over the morning light. In summer, a ceiling or pedestal fan is essential.

A bedside with room for a lamp, a few books and fresh flowers, plus a wall light for reading in bed. Image via Pinterest.

A Roman blind softens the window, with a lamp at the desk for a second layer of light in the evening. Image via Sean Symington Interior Design.

The finishing layer

This is where a guest room starts to feel personal. Set out fresh towels on the bed or a bench, with a hand towel and face washer. A luggage rack or upholstered bench keeps suitcases off the floor. Add a small jug of foliage from the garden, an artwork you love, and the Wi-Fi details written on a card. None of it is expensive, and all of it tells your guests they were expected.

A simple artwork and a few stems of garden foliage in a glass vase are sometimes all a room needs to feel personal. Image via Pinterest.

A twin room, perfectly guest ready. Image via Sean Symington Interior Design.

A well-considered guest room isn’t about how much you put in it. It’s about anticipating what someone needs before they have to ask.
