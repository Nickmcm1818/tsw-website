Banquette Seating: A Roundup of Our Favourites

A banquette is one of the most considered ways to seat a room. It makes the most of a corner or a window, seats more people than a set of chairs, and turns a dining space into somewhere people actually want to stay. Done well, it looks as though it had always been part of the house.

So, what makes a good one? Here is a roundup of some of our favourites, and what makes each one work.

A free-standing banquette custom-made to fit the space in a bold blue and white stripe makes the most of this dining alcove, with a pendant hung low over the table and simple table and rush-seat chairs. Image via Pinterest

The Kitchen Nook

A banquette is at its best in a kitchen, where it turns a spare corner into the place the household gathers. Tucked into the corner, it frees up floor space and keeps the table out of the main thoroughfare. A fixed seat also means one less chair to pull in and out, and a comfortable place to sit while dinner is being made.

The Dining Room

In a dining room, a banquette brings softness to what is often the most formal room in the house. A long upholstered seat along one wall with chairs opposite gives a relaxed, café-like feel and seats a crowd comfortably. Choose a table on a pedestal or trestle base so people can slide in without catching a leg.

A long banquette with chairs opposite keeps this dining room relaxed and seats a crowd without dominating the space. Image via Pinterest

The Window Bay

A bay or window seat is a natural home for a banquette. Built to follow the shape of the window, it makes use of a space that is otherwise hard to furnish, and the view does the rest. This is also where storage under the seat earns its keep.

A bespoke seat in a gorgeous duck egg leather follows the curve of the bay and makes the most of this light-filled window. Image via Mina Staples Interiors

Set beneath a run of windows, a soft blue banquette keeps the view in full focus. Image via Pinterest

Living Spaces and Entries

Banquettes are not only for eating. In a family room or an entry, a built-in seat gives you somewhere to sit, read or take your shoes off, with storage beneath. It is a quietly practical way to add seating to a room without adding furniture.

A built-in timber bench with a tan leather cushion and storage beneath adds seating to this kitchen area and makes it a considered part of the joinery. Image via Georgina Wilson & Associates.

What Makes a Good Banquette

The best ones share a few things. Comfort comes first: a seat height of around 45cm, a seat depth of 45 to 50cm and a gentle rake to the back make the difference between a seat people perch on and one they stay at. Fabric matters just as much. Choose a hard-wearing, wipeable fabric or leather, particularly in a kitchen, and consider piping or a contrast trim to give the banquette some definition.

Finish with cushions to soften the back, and a pendant hung low over the table to bring the whole nook together. A banquette is built to fit its room, so it is worth planning early. Custom upholstery often carries lead times of eight to twelve weeks.
